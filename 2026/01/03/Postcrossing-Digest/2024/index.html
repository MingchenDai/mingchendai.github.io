Postcrossing Digest 2024

Posted on 2026-01-03 Edited on 2026-03-06 In Post Word count in article: 1.1k Reading time ≈ 4 mins.

Here is a digest of the postcrossing postcards I’ve sent in 2024. My postcard activities can be found on Collections/Postcards.

2024 Overview

Sending and Receiving Summary

This year I create my Postcrossing account and begin to send and receive postcards with postcrossers all over the world. Before joining Postcrossing, however, I also sent some postcards but limited to my friends and family members while collecting some interesting stamps and postal marks, but it seems that few of them are interested in collecting, or even receiving postcards.

I was in Hong Kong, nearing the end of a family trip. While everyone else was caught up in the rush of shopping, I found myself in a small, tucked-away shop in Kowloon, hunting for the perfect view of the city skyline.

In that time I just graduated from high school, expecting the life in university. In the first card I wrote the weather in Hong Kong and my excitement about the upcoming university life, maybe childish though. After a month the postcard arrived its destination in USA with a return message from the receiver. That’s the beginning of my postcard journey.

I check my sent postcards in 2024 again when waiting for my breakfast. It reminds me of excited experience of 2024, from Guangming Peak in Huangshan to Central Street in Hong Kong, from beautiful natural sceneries in Zhangjiajie to modern urban landscapes in Shenzhen, and from trips with high school classmates in Yancheng to cozy afternoons alone in a lakeside café in Shanghai. Each postcard carries a piece of my journey, a snapshot of a moment in time that I wanted to share with someone across the world.

2024, But in Numbers

This year I sent 16 postcards to 12 countries and received 20 from 8 through Postcrossing. Postcards from Germany are the majority, accounting for 60% of all received postcards, followed by Poland and USA. Germans are really postcard enthusiasts! However, most of my sent postcards are to Russia accounting for 25% followed by those to UK.

Afternoons, Postcards, and Lotus Flowers

Receiving postcards is an enjoyable thing. Near my university’s postal office sits a Luckin Coffee by the edge of a lake. This past summer, my favorite afternoons were spent there: a latte in one hand, butter cookies on the table, and a fresh stack of postcards to read.

Watching the lotus flowers on the water while reading a stranger’s greeting from halfway across the globe makes the world feel remarkably small and intimate. Even a simple greeting acts as a bridge between lives that would otherwise never touch.

Search for Sincerity

Of course there are some disappointments. Losing postcards is not included, however, since postal services are usually not reliable in most countries and I usually treat missing cards as a favour of fate. What is harder to swallow is a lack of effort. I’ve received cards with nothing but a scrawled “Hello,” or worse, long paragraphs that were clearly printed by a computer or generated by AI. To me, these feel hollow.

I do not expect receiving a masterpiece or a novel - not everyone can be Shakespeare. I do not care the length of the message, the neatness of handwriting or the artistic design - although I usually write long messages and choose postcards I appreciate. It would make my day if I see a long heartfelt letter, a beautiful drawing or a well-designed card, but a short and simple greeting on a simple white card is also fine. What I really appreciate in postcard exchange is the emotional communication but not the material form. A postcard is just a medium, but the feelings behind it are what matters.

One year in, I’ve realized that I’m not just collecting paper and stamps—I’m collecting moments of sincerity.
